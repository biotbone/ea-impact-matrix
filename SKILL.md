# Enterprise Architecture Project Impact Matrix

## What this produces

A workbook that scores every proposed initiative on the same weighted
dimensions, so a sequencing decision can be defended in an EA forum, plus
optionally a discussion report for the working session.

The workbook is generated by `scripts/build_impact_matrix.py` from a JSON
config. Do not hand-build the spreadsheet cell by cell. Write the config,
run the script, recalculate, verify.

## The failure mode this exists to prevent

The commonest impact matrix gives every initiative the same ratings. Same
High, same Medium, same "Requires coordination" note, same generic owners.
It looks like analysis and contains none, because a matrix where every row
scores identically cannot rank anything.

**If two initiatives end up with the same score profile, the assessment is
not finished.** Go back to the source documents and find what actually
separates them. Something always does.

## Method

### Step 0. Confirm the inputs

The workbook is only as good as the documents behind it. Before scoring, check
the intake list in `references/required-documents.md` (print it any time with
`--intake`). Five documents are required:

| Code | Document | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| `PROPOSAL` | Proposal, business case or statement of work | Each initiative |
| `ESTIMATE` | Cost and effort estimate: line items, hours by role, duration | Each initiative |
| `TARGET_STATE` | Target-state architecture, principles and strategy | Portfolio |
| `LENS` | Sponsor confirmation of the prioritization lens | Portfolio |
| `FUNDING` | Funding envelope, or a written "not yet defined" | Portfolio |

Eight more are recommended or optional. Each one improves a named part of the
workbook (the reference says which: current state, cost baseline, incident
history, security findings, ownership, capacity, contracts, prior decisions).

Register every document in `documents` with its type, the initiative it covers
(`ALL` for the whole portfolio), version or date, who provided it and its
status: Received, Requested, Not available or Not applicable. The Read Me sheet
turns that register into a coverage table that shows what is still missing.

A missing required document does not stop the work, it changes it. Hold the
initiative or score conservatively, log the gap on `05 Open Questions`, and
send the client the intake request printed by `--intake`. `--check` warns on
every required document that is not Received. The plain `sources` list from
earlier versions is still accepted and printed on the Read Me.

### Step 1. Read every source document before scoring anything

Proposal decks, migration plans, architecture documents, existing matrices.
Read all of them. Scores must trace to evidence, and the evidence column
needs a document and section reference, not a vibe.

Extract per initiative: cost, duration, resource mix, stated benefits,
stated risks, scope boundaries, any deadline, and every question the
proposal itself asks the client. Those questions are already known gaps and
belong on the open questions sheet.

### Step 2. Confirm the prioritization lens with the sponsor first

Weights before scores, never the other way round. Picking the lens after
seeing the results is how you get an answer you wanted rather than one you
can defend.

Ask directly: reduce run cost, retire deadline risk, deliver visible wins
fastest, or improve security posture? Presets are on the `01 Scoring Model`
sheet and in `LENS_PRESETS` in the script:

| Preset | Value | Cost | Time | Risk | Security | Confidence | Strategy |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `balanced` | 20% | 20% | 20% | 10% | 10% | 10% | 10% |
| `run_cost` | 10% | 35% | 15% | 10% | 5% | 15% | 10% |
| `deadline_risk` | 10% | 10% | 35% | 20% | 5% | 10% | 10% |
| `delivery_speed` | 15% | 10% | 15% | 10% | 5% | 35% | 10% |
| `security` | 10% | 10% | 10% | 20% | 35% | 5% | 10% |

Raise delivery confidence when the funding envelope is undefined. Work that
lands quickly and predictably is worth more when nobody has agreed a number
yet.

Record who agreed the lens and when (`portfolio.lens_approved_by` and
`lens_approved_date`), and the risk threshold they accept
(`portfolio.risk_threshold`, default 9). A lens nobody signed is a lens
nobody will defend.

### Step 3. Score each initiative independently, 1 to 5

Seven dimensions, anchors on the `01 Scoring Model` sheet. Rules:

- Score against evidence. A 3 with a named source beats a 5 with none.
- Where no evidence exists, score conservatively and log the gap. Never
  guess upward to be agreeable.
- Score below neutral where a proposal is weaker than it claims. A proposal
  that replaces a managed service with self-managed servers scores low on
  security even though the deck lists security as a benefit, because the
  team now owns patching and key handling. Those cells are the most valuable
  in the workbook.
- Delivery confidence includes readiness, not only technical difficulty. A
  proven pattern still scores low if the sponsor, the skills or the capacity
  are not in place.
- Write the assessment as an argument, not a label. "Medium" says nothing;
  "monitoring covers only the primary site today, so failures at the
  secondary site are invisible during an incident" says something a reviewer
  can dispute.

### Step 4. Add the money

Pull cost and duration from the proposal. Compute score per $100k alongside
the weighted score, because the two will disagree and the disagreement is
the useful output. A high-score, low-efficiency item is usually a must-do
that should be funded on a separate line rather than competing with
discretionary work.

Check the proposal's arithmetic while you are in there. Totals that do not
match their line items, decimal separator errors, and hour counts implying
more than one FTE per resource are all common and all embarrassing in an
SOW. List them.

### Step 5. Find the cross-project dependencies

The single most valuable thing this artifact adds over a proposal deck. Look
for:

- **Enabling.** One project makes another measurable or possible. A cost
  visibility project usually enables every savings claim in the portfolio.
- **Blocking.** Two projects touch the same resource and cannot run
  concurrently.
- **Technical.** Shared platform, tenant, or naming convention that must be
  agreed once rather than twice.
- **Capacity.** The same named role committed full time to two projects. Add
  up the hours per role across the portfolio and check the peak.
- **Commercial.** Consumption-billed platforms where two projects both
  increase volume against one contract. Model them together.

### Step 6. Build the wave plan

Waves need an entry condition, not just an order. If the envelope is
undefined, structure it as approval tranches so a small first tranche can be
approved while finance is still deciding.

Enablers go first even when they score below the big items. Measurement
before optimization: a portfolio that cannot evidence its savings has not
saved anything.

### Step 7. Log what is missing

Every unanswered question, with the decision it blocks and who can answer
it. Group by owner at the end so the PM can chase efficiently. Mark status:
`Open`, `In progress`, `Answered`, `Mitigated`.

### Step 8. Record the decision and the benefit owner

Scores recommend; a forum decides. Log each decision on `07 Decision Log`:
the outcome (Approved, Approved with conditions, Deferred, Held, Rejected),
the conditions, the named decider and date, and the ADR or minutes that
record it. For every approved initiative name the benefits owner and the date
the KPIs are checked against the business case. Set a next review date, since
a portfolio scored once and never revisited drifts away from the plan.

## Required sheets

| Sheet | Contents |
|---|---|
| `00 Read Me` | Purpose, sheet guide, cell conventions, the rules above |
| `01 Scoring Model` | Weights, lens presets, 1 to 5 anchors |
| `02 Portfolio Summary` | Roll-up: score, rank, cost, score per $100k, wave |
| `03 Sequencing` | Waves with entry conditions, cross-dependencies, capacity |
| `04 Risk Register` | All risks, likelihood x impact, sorted descending |
| `05 Open Questions` | Gaps, the decision each blocks, owner, status |
| `06 Savings Case` | Payback model. Required whenever the lens is cost |
| `07 Decision Log` | Forum decisions, conditions, named decider, date, ADR reference |
| `P1`..`Pn` | One per initiative, eight sections each |

## Project sheet layout

Cell addresses are fixed because the summary and savings sheets reference
them. Do not move these:

| Cell | Contents |
|---|---|
| `B5` | Project ID |
| `B6` | Project name |
| `B10` | Estimated cost (USD) |
| `B11` | Estimated duration (months) |
| `B14` | Recommended wave |
| `B20:B26` | The seven dimension scores, in order |
| `D27` | Weighted priority score |

Sections, in order: 1 Project profile, 2 Weighted impact assessment,
3 Scope in and out, 4 Dependencies, 5 Key risks, 6 Success KPIs,
7 EA governance gates, 8 Open questions, 9 Traceability and benefits
(optional fields).

## Usage

```bash
# blank fill-in template with 8 project slots and one worked example
python scripts/build_impact_matrix.py --template -o EA_Impact_Matrix_TEMPLATE.xlsx

# starting config to edit
python scripts/build_impact_matrix.py --dump-config portfolio.json

# print the documents the matrix needs, as Markdown, with an intake request
python scripts/build_impact_matrix.py --intake

# check a config against the method's rules, write nothing
python scripts/build_impact_matrix.py portfolio.json --check

# build from a completed config (runs the same check first)
python scripts/build_impact_matrix.py portfolio.json -o Impact_Matrix_v1.xlsx
```

`--check` stops the build on errors (invalid scores, weights that do not sum
to 1, bad statuses) and warns on broken rules of the method: identical score
profiles, scores without an assessment and evidence, unnamed owners, red risks
without an owner, KPIs without a baseline, no recorded lens approver or review
date, required documents not received. Add `--strict` to fail on warnings too. Run it before you share the file.

**Always recalculate afterwards.** openpyxl writes formulas without cached
values, so the file reads back as empty to anything that is not Excel:

```bash
soffice --headless --convert-to xlsx --outdir recalculated Impact_Matrix_v1.xlsx
```

Then verify with `data_only=True` that the scores, ranks and totals are the
numbers you expected. A clean recalc proves the formulas evaluate, not that
they are right.

## Config shape

```jsonc
{
  "portfolio": { "name": "...", "version": "1.0", "date": "...",
                 "lens": "run_cost", "purpose": ["..."],
                 "lens_approved_by": "...", "lens_approved_date": "...",   // optional
                 "risk_appetite": "...", "risk_threshold": 9,
                 "next_review": "..." },
  "weights": { "business_value": 0.10, "cost_optimization": 0.35,
               "time_criticality": 0.15, "risk_reduction": 0.10,
               "security_compliance": 0.05, "delivery_confidence": 0.15,
               "strategic_alignment": 0.10 },
  "weight_rationale": ["one line per dimension, why it carries this weight"],
  "projects": [{
    "id": "P1", "sheet": "P1", "name": "...",
    "sponsor": "...", "architecture_owner": "...", "delivery_owner": "...",
    "cost": 84000, "cost_note": "...", "months": 3, "duration_note": "...",
    "deadline": "...", "status": "...", "wave": "Wave 1", "basis": "...",
    "scores": { "business_value": 4, "...": 3 },
    "assessments": { "business_value": { "assessment": "...", "owner": "...",
                                         "evidence": "doc, section" } },
    "scope_in": ["..."], "scope_out": ["..."],
    "dependencies": [["Type","Depends on","Nature","Owner","Criticality"]],
    "risks": [["Risk","Medium","Critical","Mitigation","Owner",
               "Mitigate","Low","High"]],   // items 6 to 8 optional: response, residual L, residual I
    "kpis": [["KPI","Baseline","Target","Source","By when"]],
    "gates": [["Met","Evidence","Reviewer"]],
    "questions": ["..."],
    "capability": "...", "principles": "...", "gap_closed": "...",   // optional
    "benefit_owner": "...", "benefits_review": "...", "standards_refs": "..."
  }],
  "waves": [["Wave 1","projects","window",59100,"why","entry condition"]],
  "cross_dependencies": [["P4","P3","Enabling","Strong","what it means","Owner"]],
  "resources": [["Role","Committed","Peak","","Constraint",""]],
  "open_questions": [["P5","Area","Question","Why it matters","Owner","Open"]],
  "documents": [["PROPOSAL","P1","Proposal deck v3","2026-01-08","Sponsor","Received"]],
  "decisions": [["P1","Approved with conditions","...","Named person","2026-11-03","ADR-014"]],
  "savings": { "P1": { "annual_run_cost": 0, "expected_saving": 0, "note": "..." } },
  "summary_notes": ["the specific reading for this portfolio, naming initiatives"]
}
```

Likelihood and impact must be one of `Low`, `Medium`, `High`, `Critical`.
Response must be `Mitigate`, `Accept`, `Transfer` or `Avoid`. Decisions must
be one of `Approved`, `Approved with conditions`, `Deferred`, `Held`,
`Rejected`. The sheets offer these as dropdowns.
Gate status must be one of `Met`, `Partial`, `Not started`, `Not met`,
`Not applicable`. Weights must sum to 1.0; the script warns if they do not.

Fill `gates` with ten entries in the order listed on the sheet. Leave
`scores` out entirely for a slot that has not been assessed yet, and the
sheet renders as a blank template with yellow input cells.

## Writing the discussion report

When a working session is coming, produce a companion document. Structure:

1. Why we are meeting, with one takeaway stated up front
2. How the scoring works and why each weight sits where it does
3. Where each initiative landed, plus what moved when the lens was applied
4. Recommended sequencing and the reasoning behind the order
5. One brief per initiative: what it is, why it sits where it does, what we
   need and from whom, what to watch out for
6. Dependencies that constrain the plan, plus estimate corrections
7. Red risks (at or above the risk threshold, 9 by default)
8. Decisions needed in the session, with owner and deadline
9. Who owes us what, grouped by owner

Write it to be argued with. Name the scores that are deliberately lower than
the proposal implies and invite challenge on them. A session that changes
three scores is a better session than one that rubber-stamps all seven.

## Things to get right

- **Owners are people, not roles.** "Security" approves nothing. A name does.
- **Every KPI needs a baseline.** A target with no baseline cannot be
  measured, and "TBC" in the baseline column is an honest answer that belongs
  on the open questions sheet.
- **Held is not dropped.** An initiative with no business case gets held
  pending one, with the specific data named. Say what would unhold it.
- **Surface the uncomfortable findings.** Security regressions, uncosted
  consumption charges, arithmetic errors, and scope that does not match the
  estimate. The client's own people will find them; better it comes from us.
- **Never claim a saving without a baseline.** Put it on `06 Savings Case`
  with the baseline blank and let the empty payback column make the point.
- **Run `--check` before you share the workbook.** It catches the failures
  that make a matrix indefensible: identical profiles, scores with no
  evidence, required documents missing, unnamed owners, red risks with no
  owner, KPIs with no baseline.
- **A recommendation is not a decision.** Log what the forum decided, on what
  conditions and who decided it. Otherwise the matrix proves analysis and
  nothing else.

## Standards alignment

The method is built to sit inside the governance a client already runs. It
follows TOGAF (work packages and transition architectures, prioritization by
cost, benefit and risk, architecture compliance and decision records) and
COBIT 2019 (portfolio, benefits, risk and resource objectives). The crosswalk
is in `references/standards-mapping.md`. Use the client's own terms when they
use them: an initiative is a work package and a wave is a transition
architecture.
